# UI, visualization and qualitative evidence specification

## Visual identity
Use DEC blue `#3B99D4`, teal `#0F8F8C`, green `#91C852`, deep navy `#0F172A`, white/light neutral backgrounds and pale mint. Amber is for attention; red is reserved for critical/blocking evidence. Never rely on color alone.

Use `assets/brand/dec-logo.png` discreetly at the upper-left of the application shell. Use the partner strip only as a small thin footer/credits element; never as a large banner.

## Visualization principles
- Every visual must answer a pilot decision question.
- Show counts/denominators with percentages.
- Distinguish `NOT TESTED`, `N/A` and no submission/evidence.
- Do not average Practical Check and Quality Review into a common metric.
- Do not create a single overall readiness score.
- Prefer accessible stacked bars, labelled matrices/heatmaps, compact status tables, evidence-coverage bars and trend lines only where time matters.
- Avoid 3D, decorative gauges, overloaded card walls and unnecessary animation.
- All charts need readable labels, keyboard-accessible surrounding controls and a tabular/text alternative when appropriate.

## Qualitative evidence
Do not hide open-ended evidence behind a word cloud.

Provide:
- searchable exact excerpts;
- filter by course/system, domain, indicator/source question, rating/result, category, blocker status and Decision Horizon;
- dedicated views for KEEP, Priority Improvement and Possible Blocker;
- practical workplace-use examples;
- confusing/difficult screen/activity evidence;
- recommendations and support needs;
- source record/tester traceability where internal users are authorized;
- one-click/promote-to-finding flow that links original source records rather than retyping evidence.

If AI-assisted thematic summaries are added later, label them clearly, keep the source excerpts accessible and never allow AI summarization to make the readiness decision.
